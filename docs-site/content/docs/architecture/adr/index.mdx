Architecture Decision Records (ADRs) capture important architectural decisions alongside their context, rationale, and consequences. They exist so future contributors can understand not just _what_ the codebase does, but _why_ it is structured the way it is.

## What is an ADR?

An ADR is a short document that answers three questions about a significant architectural decision:

1. **Context** — What situation prompted this decision? What constraints or forces were at play?
2. **Decision** — What exactly was decided?
3. **Consequences** — What becomes easier, harder, or different as a result?

ADRs are written _after_ a decision is made (or while it is being finalized) and are never deleted — even superseded decisions are preserved with a `Deprecated` status so the history remains intact.

## ADR Statuses

| Status | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| **Proposed** | Under discussion — not yet adopted |
| **Accepted** | Decision adopted and in effect |
| **Deprecated** | Superseded by a later ADR |
| **Rejected** | Considered but not adopted |
